The house

A press you can talk to.

H.D. Publishing is an independent press in the Pacific Northwest. We exist for the reader who wants a real person on the other end of an order, the author who has a book in them and no way to get it printed, and the neighbourhood of businesses and readers that a book ought to serve first.

Most books today pass through a platform. You upload a file or you click a button, and somewhere a warehouse does the rest. Nobody reads the manuscript. Nobody knows your name. If something goes wrong with the order, you talk to a form.

We built this press for the people that arrangement leaves behind. The author who has always meant to write the book and needs a shape, a schedule, and someone to answer the phone. The reader who would rather buy from a shop that can tell them why the book is worth their evening. The local business that wants its story, its history, or its expertise between covers, produced by people who live where it does.

So the press is small on purpose and personal by design. Every author works with a person who knows their book by name. Every order is handled by a person, not a queue. And when a book needs an editor, a designer, a printer, or a bookshop, we look in the Pacific Northwest first, and as the press grows, the people who work here are hired here.

What we publish follows from that. Original nonfiction that lays its evidence out plainly enough for a reader to judge for themselves. Classic texts restored to the words of their first printing for readers tired of quiet modern edits. And, through the Writer, the books of first-time authors who would never have reached print any other way.

Personal, local, ours

Three promises we can keep because we are small.

A person answers

Write to us and a person replies, usually the same day. Authors get a named contact for the whole ninety days and after. Readers with an order question get an answer, not a ticket number.

Local first

Pacific Northwest authors, readers, and businesses come first in what we publish and who we work with. Editors, designers, printers, and shops close to home get the first call for every job they can do.

Books for the people around us

A town's history, a family's story, a regional practice's guide, a shop's cookbook. Books that a national platform would never notice and that a neighbourhood will keep for years.

Standards

What the seal means.

  • Human authorship. Our authors write their own books. Where we use AI at all, it is for structure and critique, and it is never used to generate the text you read.
  • Everything cited. In our nonfiction, every fact, quotation, and date is cited to its source by date and index. Interpretation is marked as interpretation.
  • First-printing texts. Era Editions follow the book's first printing. Variants are noted; nothing is silently modernised. The editorial apparatus is marked as ours.
  • Paper first. Every title is designed as a 6 × 9 book before it is anything else. The ebook follows the print.
  • Open type. Interiors are set in openly licensed typefaces we can name in the colophon and ship with the files.
  • Small, on purpose. A handful of titles a year, each one finished. We would rather publish less and stand behind all of it.
  • No bought reviews, no bought rankings. Not on any platform, under any name.
  • Rights we can show. Every classic we reprint is in the public domain in the territories where we sell it, and the source of the text is named in the book.
The seal

H and D, either side of a rule.

The mark is a printer's seal: the two initials set in Trebuchet, divided by a thin claret rule, inside a double ring, with the word PUBLISHING on the lower arc. The rule is the only colour. It is drawn from the column rule of a printed page, and it is the one thing the house will not print without.

Press and trade may use the files below unaltered. Keep clear space of at least half the ring's diameter around the mark, and never scale the full seal below 120 pixels; use the standard cut for small sizes. Colours: ink #1C1A17, ivory #F4EFE6, claret #7B2D26.
